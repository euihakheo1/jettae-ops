## AutoTSG: Learning and Synthesis for Incident Troubleshooting
Manish Shetty, Chetan Bansal, Sai Pramod Upadhyayula, Arjun Radhakrishna, Anurag Gupta (Microsoft) — ESEC/FSE 2022 (Industry track), Proc. 30th ACM ESEC/FSE, pp. 1477-1488, DOI 10.1145/3540250.3558958 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13457 — verified=True
- idea: A large empirical study of more than 4,000 TSGs, plus feedback from more than 400 on-call engineers, led to a taxonomy of TSG quality issues. The categories, as a share of OCE feedback, were: Completeness 32.2%, Broken Link 13.3%, Correctness 11.2%, Readability 10.3%, UX 10.1%, Empty 7.2%, Up-to-dateness 6.5% and Relevance 4.0%. The abstract describes TSGs as 'siloed, unstructured, and often incomplete'. AutoTSG then uses ML and program synthesis to turn manual TSGs into executable workflows. On 50 TSGs it scored 0.89 accuracy at identifying statements, and parsing reached 0.94 precision and 0.91 recall. On the incident count: the Microsoft Research page says the TSGs map to 17K incidents, while the arXiv paper says '1000s' and that the exact number cannot be disclosed.
- JETTAE: APPLIES: cite it as the founding evidence that runbook defects are systemic and can be classified. Its taxonomy (Completeness, Broken Link, Correctness, Up-to-dateness and so on) can be mapped directly onto JETTAE's fixed rule set, which works as an executable version of such a defect taxonomy. DIFFERS: AutoTSG synthesizes technical commands from English TSGs. JETTAE extracts the organizational response path (owner, approver, fallback channel, deadline, regulatory report step) from Korean free text and audits it with LLM-free rules instead of executing it.

## Recommending Root-Cause and Mitigation Steps for Cloud Incidents using Large Language Models
Toufique Ahmed, Supriyo Ghosh, Chetan Bansal, Thomas Zimmermann, Xuchao Zhang, Saravan Rajmohan (UC Davis / Microsoft) — ICSE 2023 (2023 IEEE/ACM 45th Int. Conf. on Software Engineering), pp. 1737-1749, DOI 10.1109/ICSE48619.2023.00149 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03797 — verified=True
- idea: The abstract calls this the 'first large-scale study' of LLMs for recommending root causes and mitigation steps, on more than 40,000 Microsoft incidents. It compares zero-shot, fine-tuned and multi-task settings across GPT-3.x models (Curie, Codex, Davinci, code-davinci-002/GPT-3.5), with RoBERTa and CodeBERT as baselines. The only inputs are the incident title and summary as they stood when the incident was created. Actual incident owners did a human evaluation.
- JETTAE: APPLIES: cite it as the origin of LLMs for incident management. DIFFERS: JETTAE deliberately keeps the LLM out of live root-cause generation, where hallucination costs the most. It works before the incident, on runbook readiness. The LLM only drafts fix sentences, which become a new graph version only after a human approves them.

## Automatic Root Cause Analysis via Large Language Models for Cloud Incidents (RCACopilot)
Yinfang Chen, Huaibing Xie, Minghua Ma, Yu Kang, Xin Gao, Liu Shi, Yunjie Cao, Xuedong Gao, Hao Fan, Ming Wen, Jun Zeng, Supriyo Ghosh, Xuchao Zhang, Chaoyun Zhang, Qingwei Lin, Saravan Rajmohan, Dongmei Zhang, Tianyin Xu (Microsoft / UIUC et al.) — EuroSys 2024 (19th European Conf. on Computer Systems, Athens), pp. 674-688, DOI 10.1145/3627703.3629553 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15778 — verified=True
- idea: Incoming incidents are matched by alert type to incident-specific handlers, which collect multi-source runtime diagnostics. Only afterwards does an LLM predict the root-cause category and write an explanation. RCA accuracy reaches up to 0.766 on a year of Microsoft incidents, and the diagnostic-collection part has been used at Microsoft for more than 4 years.
- JETTAE: APPLIES: the same reliability pattern, with deterministic structure first and the LLM last. JETTAE's graph and rule engine play the role of RCACopilot's handlers, and the LLM only writes text on top. DIFFERS: RCACopilot's handlers are hand-built by engineers and used during incidents. JETTAE builds its graph automatically from prose runbooks, grounds every field to a source span, and validates it before any incident happens.

## LLexus: an AI agent system for incident management
Pedro Las-Casas, Alok Gautum Kumbhare, Rodrigo Fonseca, Sharad Agarwal (Microsoft) — ACM SIGOPS Operating Systems Review, 2024 (Aug 2024; DOI 10.1145/3689051.3689056) — https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/llexus-an-ai-agent-system-for-incident-management/ — verified=True
- idea: LLexus has an offline planner and an online executor. The planner is an LLM agent run in rounds with a human in the loop. It is triggered when a TSG is created or edited and turns the TSG into an executable plan: a BPMN-inspired flowchart with action, condition and event nodes, solid edges for execution flow and dashed edges for data flow. Engineers audit the plan. The TSG stays the source of truth: if the plan is wrong, the engineer fixes the TSG and reruns the planner rather than editing the plan. When an incident occurs, the executor follows the pre-generated plan without LLM planning, which cuts LLM cost and the room for hallucination. It still calls an LLM inside individual 'semantic tool' steps, such as interpreting log exceptions, but never across steps. The paper's analysis of one team's 54 TSGs found a median of 815 words, the largest above 5,500 words, Flesch-Kincaid grade levels of up to about 20 years of education, a median of 8 edits, and a median update interval of 19 days.
- JETTAE: APPLIES: this is the closest prior art and the strongest citation for JETTAE's core philosophy: front-load the LLM with a human in the loop, then run deterministically. Its 'TSG is the source of truth' rule parallels JETTAE's source-span grounding. JETTAE's LLM-off check, score and replay mode goes further than LLexus, whose executor still uses LLMs inside semantic steps. DIFFERS: LLexus automates the technical steps. JETTAE models the people and governance layer (owner, approver, departed contacts, escalation and notification edges, the Korean 24h/72h report deadlines) and adds a 0-100 readiness score, mock-incident rehearsal and versioned human-approved patches. JETTAE asks whether the human response path will hold, not how to execute it.

## Nissist: An Incident Mitigation Copilot based on Troubleshooting Guides
Kaikai An, Fangkai Yang, Junting Lu, Liqun Li, Zhixing Ren, Hao Huang, Lu Wang, Pu Zhao, Yu Kang, Hua Ding, Qingwei Lin, Saravan Rajmohan, Dongmei Zhang, Qi Zhang (Microsoft / Peking Univ.) — ECAI 2024 (Frontiers in AI and Applications vol. 392, DOI 10.3233/FAIA241032); arXiv Feb 2024, v2 May 2024 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17531 — verified=True
- idea: LLMs reformat unstructured, incomplete TSGs and past mitigation discussions into a structured knowledge base. A multi-agent copilot (intent interpreter, node retriever and selector, action planner, post-processor) then gives the on-call engineer stepwise actions. In a study with 20 OCEs, each handling 5 incidents, the success rate without human intervention was 77.19% for simple incidents and 52.63% for hard ones. Time to mitigate dropped by 98.93% and 94.85% respectively compared with manual mitigation. The stated motivation is on-call fatigue, especially among new-hire OCEs.
- JETTAE: APPLIES: cite it for the problem statement (unstructured TSGs cause on-call fatigue and new-hire struggle) and for the multi-agent extraction design. DIFFERS: Nissist is an in-incident retrieval copilot. JETTAE is a pre-incident auditor. Its knowledge structure is a span-grounded, versioned graph in which nothing is invented, rather than a retrieval knowledge base, and its findings come from deterministic rules rather than LLM answers.

## FLASH: A Workflow Automation Agent for Diagnosing Recurring Incidents
Xuchao Zhang, Tanish Mittal, Chetan Bansal, Rujia Wang, Minghua Ma, Zhixin Ren, Hao Huang, Saravan Rajmohan (Microsoft Research) — Microsoft Research publication page, Oct 2024 (no peer-reviewed venue found) — https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/flash-a-workflow-automation-agent-for-diagnosing-recurring-incidents/ — verified=True
- idea: Status supervision tracks the workflow's current status and splits complex instructions into simpler, status-dependent steps. Hindsight integration has the LLM generate hindsight from past failures, which feeds into reflection. On 250 production Microsoft incidents (the project page says 'over 250') across 5 workflow-automation scenarios, it scores 13.2% higher accuracy on average than state-of-the-art agents. In the AIOpsLab paper, a simplified reimplementation of FLASH (the original was not public) was the best agent, at 59.32% overall.
- JETTAE: APPLIES: explicit status tracking maps to JETTAE's deterministic rehearsal state machine over the operations graph, which walks a mock incident step by step. DIFFERS: FLASH's hindsight is reflection the LLM writes itself. JETTAE's learning loop is human-approved patches stored as rollback-able graph versions, so every change has an auditable approver. Cite with medium confidence on venue, since only a Microsoft Research publication page exists. If quoting the 59.32% AIOpsLab figure, say it came from a simplified reimplementation.

## StepFly: Agentic Troubleshooting Guide Automation for Incident Diagnosis
Jiayi Mao, Liqun Li, Yanjie Gao, Zegang Peng, Shilin He, Chaoyun Zhang, Si Qin, Samia Khalid, Qingwei Lin, Saravan Rajmohan, Sitaram Lanka, Dongmei Zhang (Microsoft) — FSE 2026 Research Papers (PACMSE vol. 3, FSE issue, DOI 10.1145/3808143); arXiv Oct 2025, v2 Apr 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10074 — verified=True
- idea: An empirical study of 92 real TSGs from 9 teams found five issue categories: Clarity and Precision 37.4%, Database Instruction 27.2%, Data Flow 20.4%, Presentation and Structure 9.9% and Control Flow 5.1% (Cohen's kappa 0.78). TSG Mentor, an LLM-based quality checker, flags issues with precision 0.85, recall 0.78 and F1 0.81. An LLM extracts an execution DAG of step nodes plus Start and End, with conditional and unconditional edges, and TSG authors review it. Extraction scored overall F1 94.89%. A DAG-guided scheduler and executor with memory runs independent steps in parallel, reaching 94.38% success on GPT-4.1 and cutting execution time by 32.9-70.4% on parallelizable TSGs.
- JETTAE: APPLIES: the best 2025-2026 evidence that runbook defects can be measured (its taxonomy can seed JETTAE's rule catalogue and the slide on the problem's size) and that LLM-to-graph extraction with human review is practical. DIFFERS: (1) TSG Mentor detects defects with an LLM. JETTAE uses fixed rules, so results are reproducible and still work with the LLM off. (2) StepFly assumes a DAG. JETTAE allows cycles so it can detect escalation loops. (3) JETTAE adds people, approval, deadline and regulatory nodes, plus rehearsal scenarios such as an absent owner or a messenger outage.

## TSGen: Automated Troubleshooting Guide Generation
Yi Xiao, Hongyu Zhang (Chongqing Univ.); Daniel Genkin, Chaoyun Zhang, Rujia Wang, Chetan Bansal, Bhala Ranganathan, Saravanakumar Rajmohan, Minghua Ma (Microsoft), as listed on the FSE program page — FSE 2026 Industry Papers (talk Thu 9 Jul 2026, 11:30-11:50, session 'LLM for SE 6', Montreal) — https://conf.researchr.org/details/fse-2026/fse-2026-industry-papers/37/TSGen-Automated-Troubleshooting-Guide-Generation — verified=True
- idea: An LLM pipeline generates structured TSGs from historical incident reports in three stages: filter and classify the incident data, distill core incidents for diversity and generality, and organize the knowledge into a DAG of root causes and resolutions. It supports iterative updates. On real Microsoft incidents it covers 54.8% of incidents and has about 3x the retrieval accuracy of baselines. On-call engineers rated its guides significantly higher than human-written ones; the abstract gives no numeric rating.
- JETTAE: COMPLEMENTARY: TSGen writes new guides from past incidents. JETTAE validates and repairs existing human-written runbooks before the next incident. As a roadmap item, rehearsal failures in JETTAE can point to missing runbook sections, which the LLM drafts and a human approves. No arXiv preprint or DOI was found as of 2026-10-13, so cite the FSE program page. A related but separate system, FixItFlow (arXiv 2607.13035), also generates TSGs from incidents.

## AIOpsLab: A Holistic Framework to Evaluate AI Agents for Enabling Autonomous Clouds
Yinfang Chen, Manish Shetty, Gagan Somashekar, Minghua Ma, Yogesh Simmhan, Jonathan Mace, Chetan Bansal, Rujia Wang, Saravan Rajmohan (Microsoft / IISc et al.) — MLSys 2025 (arXiv Jan 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06706 — verified=True
- idea: A framework that deploys microservice environments, injects faults, generates workloads, exports telemetry and gives agents an interface to act and be scored. It has 48 problems across detection, localization, RCA and mitigation, with 288 cases over 6 agents. The best agent (a simplified FLASH) reached 59.32% overall and 54.55% on mitigation, and GPT-3.5-with-shell recovered no failure at all (0% on mitigation). Observed failure modes include wasted steps (repeated or non-existent API calls), invalid API usage (one error repeated up to 14 times in 20 steps), telemetry dumps that flood the context, and false positives on no-fault problems.
- JETTAE: APPLIES: the pattern of fault injection plus orchestrated scoring, moved to the organizational layer. JETTAE injects mock incidents (owner absent, messenger down, concurrent failures) into the operations graph and replays them deterministically. DIFFERS: JETTAE needs no live cluster, which suits Korean SMEs and public bodies, and it scores whether the response path survives rather than agent skill. Cite the failure modes as the reason JETTAE does not let an LLM act on infrastructure.

## OpenRCA: Can Large Language Models Locate the Root Cause of Software Failures?
Junjielong Xu, Qinan Zhang, Zhiqing Zhong, Shilin He, Chaoyun Zhang, Qingwei Lin, Dan Pei, Pinjia He, Dongmei Zhang, Qi Zhang (CUHK-Shenzhen / Microsoft / Tsinghua) — ICLR 2025 — https://openreview.net/forum?id=M4qNIzQYpd — verified=True
- idea: A benchmark of 335 failures from 3 enterprise software systems with more than 68 GB of logs, metrics and traces. Even with a purpose-built RCA-agent that writes Python to query telemetry, the best model (Claude 3.5) solved only 11.34% of cases. A separate 2026 successor, 'OpenRCA 2.0: From Outcome Labels to Causal Process Supervision' (Fang et al., arXiv 2606.27154, v1 25 Jun 2026, marked 'work in progress'), tested 11 frontier LLMs on 500 instances and found the exact root-cause set in only 20.7% of cases on average.
- JETTAE: APPLIES (as negative evidence): LLM root-cause analysis on telemetry is still unreliable, so for the foreseeable future a human response path is what resolves incidents, which is the core of JETTAE's market thesis. DIFFERS: JETTAE does not try LLM diagnosis at all. It makes sure the human escalation, approval and notification path works when the AI fails. Do not mix the OpenRCA (11.34%) and OpenRCA 2.0 (20.7%) numbers, since they are different benchmarks.

## ITBench: Evaluating AI Agents across Diverse Real-World IT Automation Tasks
Saurabh Jha, Rohan R. Arora, Yuji Watanabe, ... Tianyin Xu, Lav R. Varshney, ... Nicholas C. M. Fuller, Ruchir Puri (40 authors in the PMLR version; IBM / UIUC et al.) — ICML 2025 (PMLR vol. 267, pp. 27134-27197; oral); arXiv Feb 2025 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05352 — verified=True
- idea: A benchmark of real IT-automation scenarios across SRE, CISO (compliance and security operations) and FinOps. The ICML/PMLR version has 102 scenarios: state-of-the-art agents resolve only about 11.4% of SRE scenarios, 25.2% of CISO and 25.8% of FinOps (excluding FinOps anomaly detection, where F1 is 0.35). The arXiv v1 abstract reported 13.8% SRE, 25.2% CISO and 0% FinOps on 94 scenarios.
- JETTAE: APPLIES: use 11.4% (ICML) as the headline on the pitch slide arguing that fully autonomous SRE is not ready, so humans stay in the loop and JETTAE hardens that loop. ITBench's compliance track parallels JETTAE's regulatory-deadline rules (Korean 24h breach report and 72h privacy-leak report). DIFFERS: ITBench scores agents acting on Kubernetes. JETTAE scores the readiness of the human runbook. Always state which version a number comes from.

## STRATUS: A Multi-agent System for Autonomous Reliability Engineering of Modern Clouds
Yinfang Chen, Jiaqi Pan, Jackson Clark, Yiming Su, Noah Zheutlin, Bhavya Bhavya, Rohan R. Arora, Yu Deng, Saurabh Jha, Tianyin Xu (UIUC / IBM) — NeurIPS 2025 (Main Conference Track, DOI 10.52202/085713-1671); arXiv May 2025, v2 Mar 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02009 — verified=True
- idea: Detection, diagnosis, mitigation and undo agents run inside a state machine under a formal safety specification, Transactional No-Regression (TNR): each mitigation is a transaction that can be faithfully undone, and externally visible severity never rises above its initial level. Severity is a weighted sum of active alerts, SLA violations and lost capacity (unhealthy nodes), and a crash counts as infinite. Enforcement uses a readers-writer lock (one writer at a time: mitigation or undo), sandboxed read-only detection and diagnosis agents, a stack-based mechanical Undo agent, and at most K=20 commands per transaction. A transaction commits only if the post-state is valid and severity is no worse, and otherwise aborts and undoes. Mitigation success is at least 1.5x state-of-the-art SRE agents. With GPT-4o: 69.2% vs 46.2% on AIOpsLab, and 50.0% vs 9.2% on ITBench.
- JETTAE: APPLIES: carry TNR over to runbook edits. Each human-approved patch is a transaction on the graph, committed only if the readiness score does not drop and no new rule violation appears, and otherwise rolled back. Graph versioning plays the role of the Undo agent, and extraction and analysis agents are read-only while the approved patch is the single writer. DIFFERS: STRATUS changes live infrastructure autonomously. JETTAE never touches infrastructure and requires human approval for every change, a safer fit for Korean regulated organizations.

## SREGym: A Live Benchmark for AI SRE Agents with High-Fidelity Failure Scenarios
Jackson Clark, Yiming Su, Saad Mohammad Rafid Pial, Yifang Tian, Lily Gniedziejko, Hans-Arno Jacobsen, Yinfang Chen, Tianyin Xu — arXiv preprint, May 2026 (v1 8 May, v2 13 May, v3 30 Jul 2026); no peer-reviewed venue listed — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07161 — verified=True
- idea: A live benchmark of 90 SRE problems on cloud-native stacks. It injects faults at several layers and adds ambient noise plus metastable and correlated failure modes. Frontier agents differ by up to 40% in end-to-end results.
- JETTAE: APPLIES: borrow the categories of correlated, concurrent and metastable failures as JETTAE rehearsal scenarios, such as concurrent failures that share one on-call owner, and cite the up-to-40% spread as a reason to keep scoring deterministic. DIFFERS: SREGym measures agents on live infrastructure. JETTAE replays scenarios over a document-derived graph. It is a preprint, so present it as recent evidence rather than an established result.

## Incident-Arena: Getting agents to the last nine of reliability
Andre Fu, Malik Drabla, Leon Liu, Meji Abidoye, Marek Suppa, Lata Mishra, Adnan El Assadi, Yiyuan Li — arXiv preprint, 30 Sep 2026 (2610.00648v1); no venue listed — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00648 — verified=True
- idea: 20 human-built tasks across 3 application substrates deploy real open-source applications on ephemeral Kubernetes clusters. Faults are injected from the config layer down to the images, under a sustained load profile. Functional verifiers check that system metrics stay stable and repairs are safe. Trials average 2.81M tokens and 41 turns. The abstract says frontier models score 'below 64.3%'; in the body, 64.3% is the best single model configuration's Pass@1, and that model's pooled Pass@1 is 0.59. Repeat reliability collapses: the best Pass^5 is about 0.33. Of 1,948 failures, 26.5% were repairs that did not survive a restart or the next trigger, 23.9% were declared done while the service was still out of bounds, and 21.9% changed a setting the task did not permit. The abstract groups failures as diagnosis/localization errors, incomplete repairs and unsafe regressions. Raising reasoning effort from low to max moved pooled Pass@1 only from 39.2% to 43.8%, a gain whose interval includes zero, at about 2.3x the cost per episode.
- JETTAE: APPLIES: the newest evidence, about two weeks old as of the finals prep, that LLM agents are not reliable on Kubernetes incidents. Pass^k matters most for operations, and it collapses. Use it in Q&A: 'even the best agent passes all five repeated attempts on a task only about a third of the time (Pass^5 about 0.33), so the human escalation, approval and fallback path must be ready, and that is what JETTAE checks.' The 'changed a setting the task did not permit' failure (21.9%) maps directly to JETTAE's approver and permission edges. Mark it as a non-peer-reviewed v1 preprint.
